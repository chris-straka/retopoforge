# Review, 2026-11-13: is retopoforge ready for generated creatures?

The question: does the engine and its bench turn the owner's generated
creature and prop meshes into game-ready topology, and does the bench
measure what matters for that (bending at real joints)? Earlier audit
claims were checked one by one against the code; each finding below says
what was read and whether a run confirmed it.

## The bench measured the wrong thing

1. **The bend check is a fake hinge.** `bench/deform.py:136-148`
   (`fit_rig`): the "rig" is two bones along the longest bounding-box
   axis, hinged at the box centre about the second-longest axis. On a
   creature that is a fold through the middle of the belly, not a knee or
   an elbow; on `fandisk` (a CAD part) it folds metal. Skinning
   (`skin_weights`, `deform.py:151-163`) is an analytic smoothstep across
   that plane, so the result says nothing about the mesh's loops at its
   real joints. **Confirmed by reading.**

2. **The gate lets hundreds of flipped faces through.** `deform.py:807-830`
   (`check_case`) passes a pose when flips do not exceed the baseline's
   seed-spread maximum; only where that maximum is 0 does it demand 0.
   The committed baseline (`bench/deform_baseline.json`) allows, at
   elbow135: fandisk@5000 1,220 flips, beast@5000 717, armadillo@5000
   241, nefertiti@5000 259. Stretch and volume are gated the same way
   (no worse than baseline). So "green" means "no worse than before",
   never "good". **Confirmed by reading the baseline.**

3. **Nothing measured loops at joints or poles at joints.**
   `bench/score.py` counts irregular vertices over the whole mesh
   (`irr_pct`), corner angles and surface distance; no script finds a
   joint, and no script traces edge loops. `bench/rings.py` *makes*
   ring guides from rigforge landmarks but nothing checks that a ring
   came out as a loop. **Confirmed by reading (grep over `bench/`).**

## The engine has no way to make a joint loop

4. **Guides steer the cross field only.** `rust/core/src/guides.rs`
   `tangent_near` is used in `frame_field.rs:199` to pull the field
   toward a guide's tangent. Nothing downstream asks for an edge on the
   guide. The parameterizer does have the mechanism: an edge marked
   `ConstraintU/V` (`quad_parameterizer.rs:1485-1495`) gets equal,
   integer coordinates at both ends, so it lands on an integer isoline,
   i.e. a quad edge. Only the automatic dihedral marks use it.
   Explicit `--features` get `AlignU/V` (`quad_parameterizer.rs:183-222`,
   `1499-1504`): equal but not integer, so the line is parallel to quad
   edges but not on one. The enum's docs (`quad_parameterizer.rs:122-130`)
   say full U/V pins on every marked edge collapsed yield on a box cage
   (912 integer variables); nobody tried pinning one anchor per curve.
   **Confirmed by reading.** Fix tried in the log (one integer anchor
   per ring, the rest aligned).

5. **Pole cancellation ignores joints.**
   `singularity_simplifier.rs:617-718` (`simplify`) pairs opposite-charge
   singularities by face-hop distance only (`candidates.sort_by_key(|x|
   x.hops)`) and moves them together along the shortest path
   (`cancel_pair`, :436). It takes no skeleton, and the survivors stay
   wherever the field put them, joints included. **Confirmed by
   reading;** how many poles sit at joints is now measured (bench below).

6. **Integer rounding happens in one shot.**
   `rust/solvers/src/mixed_integer.rs:614-635` (`solve_iteration`): on
   the second call it sets `threshold = max(d + 0.001, 1.0)` from the
   first free variable, and since every rounding distance `d` is at most
   0.5, every free integer variable is fixed at once. The loop in
   `quad_parameterizer.rs:1513-1527` (up to 100 iterations) therefore
   always stops after two solves. Rounding thousands of coupled variables
   independently ignores that fixing one moves the others: a thin limb
   whose unrounded girth is close to one period can round to zero and
   collapse. **One-shot rounding confirmed by reading; the thin-limb loss
   is suspected, measured by `dist_max` on the creature set.** Note:
   three earlier tries at progressive rounding were measured and dropped
   (`docs/mils-rounding-scope.md`: unrecoverable folds, a 1.7x
   `dist_max` tail), on the old static bench.

7. **Symmetry mirrors the field and snaps positions, but not the layout.**
   The audit said "positions only"; that is half right.
   `parameterizer.rs:648` mirrors the cross field before parameterizing
   (`symmetry.rs:326`), and `pipeline.rs:898` snaps output vertices
   (`symmetry.rs:437-491`). The integer layout is never mirrored, so the
   two sides can round differently, and then the snap is harmful:
   `PointGrid::nearest` is called with `max_radius = 0.0`
   (`symmetry.rs:455`), which means *unlimited* (`symmetry.rs:105-108`),
   so every vertex is averaged with its nearest mirrored vertex however
   far away it is, and a second vertex whose partner is taken is moved
   onto that partner's mirror (`symmetry.rs:463-468`), making two
   vertices coincide. On a layout that differs left and right this moves
   vertices by up to half an edge or more and can fold or collapse
   faces. **Confirmed by reading; size measured on the creature set
   (`--symmetry x` vs off).**

## Other findings

From a wider audit of the engine. The first three were re-read and
confirmed here; the rest are the audit's reading, not yet re-read, and
are listed so a bench run can confirm or clear them.

8. **A detached part is deleted, its stump capped.**
   `quad_extractor/holes.rs:256-271` (`remove_isolated_faces`) keeps only
   the largest connected piece of quads per island; `extract.rs:116-122`
   runs it in a loop with the non-manifold face eraser. If one ring of
   faces at a horn, tail or leg root is removed as non-manifold, the part
   beyond it becomes a separate piece and is thrown away, and
   `fix_holes` (`extract.rs:112`) caps the hole. **Confirmed by reading.**
9. **The coverage check cannot see short thin parts.**
   `auto_remesher/coverage.rs:646` and `:758`: the gap bar is a multiple
   of `diag / sqrt(nquads)`, a few percent of the bounding-box diagonal at
   creature budgets. A claw, ear or horn shorter than that can vanish
   without triggering the retry, and a flattened limb still counts as
   covered. **Confirmed by reading.**
10. **A total failure gets no retry.** `auto_remesher/pipeline.rs:719-724`:
    when the first attempt's output is empty the loop breaks ("the
    failed-island path owns empty outputs"), so a failed cover never
    reaches the jittered retry; the island is dropped and the CLI still
    exits 0. **Confirmed by reading.**
11. Output vertices are projected onto the coarse working mesh, never the
    input (`pipeline.rs:555`, `quad_extractor/project.rs`), so thin parts
    lose volume twice. (audit)
12. The extractor's smoothing projection has no normal check
    (`quad_extractor/project.rs:124-170`): on ears, wings and lips a vertex
    can snap to the far side and fold quads; the isotropic stage has this
    guard. (audit)
13. The 90-degree automatic sharp angle is judged on the coarse working
    mesh (`quad_parameterizer.rs:135`): a limb only four edges around has
    90-degree dihedrals and turns into a cage of integer constraints.
    (audit)
14. Separate small parts (teeth, claws) get an area-proportional share of
    one global voxel size and no quad floor; floaters are never culled.
    (audit)
15. Quad count drifts below target on creatures (the bench baseline shows
    0.56-0.69x at 1,000 quads). (audit; the creature bench calibrates
    each tool's count, so this shows as a second cut.)
16. Skeleton density zones are spheres at the bone head, radius twice the
    crease depth (`skeleton.rs`), which on a quadruped can reach the belly
    and tail base; `run.rs:200` judges creases against `sqrt(area/Q)`, not
    the edge the engine makes. (audit)
17. CLI: default 50,000 quads (Blender add-on 5,000); `--model-type` is
    stored (`auto_remesher/mod.rs:302`) but never read by the engine
    (confirmed); dropped islands still exit 0 (audit).

## The new bench

`bench/creature/` replaces the fake hinge with the creature's own rig:

- Each generated creature is cleaned once (motionforge `tripo_to_glb`,
  300k triangles: the dense source every tool cuts) and rigged once by
  the creature route (skintokens `tools/creature_route.py`, which picks
  the best of its body-read rig and the ML rig and repairs the weights).
  Hand-made animals use the artist's own rig and mesh.
- Every candidate mesh (ours, each rival, the artist's) gets that one
  rig's weights the same way (nearest surface point, interpolated, four
  bones, normalized), so only topology differs.
- Scores: weightforge's checker on the creature's own range of motion
  (`weights check --poses <rom>`); per real joint, closed edge loops
  ringing the limb within one limb radius and irregular vertices within
  one radius; flipped faces at rest (against the source) and with each
  joint bent 60 degrees four ways; two-sided surface distance and normal
  deviation; triangles at a matched budget.

Details: `bench/creature/README.md`.
